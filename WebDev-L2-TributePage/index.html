HIS STORY

A Life That Changed History

01

From prisoner to president

Nelson Mandela was a South African leader who devoted his life to challenging racial discrimination and fighting for equality.

Born in 1918 in Mvezo, South Africa, Mandela grew up during a period when racial inequality shaped nearly every part of society. He later became involved in political activism and joined the African National Congress.

Mandela became one of the leading figures opposing apartheid, the system of institutionalised racial segregation in South Africa. His political activities eventually led to his imprisonment, where he spent 27 years behind bars.

After his release in 1990, Mandela worked with other leaders to help establish a democratic South Africa. In 1994, he became the country's first democratically elected president.

Rather than seeking revenge against those who had oppressed him, Mandela promoted reconciliation and cooperation. His approach helped shape his lasting reputation as a global symbol of peace and leadership.
